Apex Predator · Drexathon

Most of a chip’s life is spent waiting.

That is not the work.

Muda Lens asks Drex the decision a black belt makes every week, then scores it against a frontier model and the rules, on cases where the answer is known.

Manjeet Singh & Vani Sethi

The decision

Four questions. One pass. A probability on each.

Which wasteThe eight Lean wastes. Waiting, defects, overprocessing, and the rest.
Which stepTwelve steps, oxidation through final test, on a power-MOSFET line.
Which fixCONWIP, re-center and SPC, DOE, 8D, the sampling plan, standard work, or the rail.
Do the lots shipRelease, hold, or rework. Under 50% confidence, an engineer gets the case.

Why the test is fair

One real fault. One decoy. All four must be right.

70 casesTen each of seven fault types. Tuned on a separate dev split. The test set was scored once.
A louder decoyAnother step is also wrong, sometimes by more. The raw biggest change is often the trap.
The same ruleEvery contender is told: product-quality risk outranks flow waste.
±15% noiseSame data, same options, to Drex, Claude Opus 5.5, and a rules baseline.
