The stakes

Guess wrong, and you either waste an hour or ship a bug.

Every failure forces a call: is it the toast, or a real fire? The two mistakes do not cost the same.

Wrong call · it was toast

You waste an engineer’s hour.

A false alarm still pages someone. They dig through the log, rerun the test, and lose an hour that wasn’t a fire.

Wrong call · it was a fire

A real bug ships.

Quarantine a real regression and the smoke alarm goes quiet — while the fire moves into production.

The insight they don’t have

The expensive part isn’t reading the logs. It’s the decision.

Most teams think the fix is a smarter AI that reads the logs. A typical LLM shrugs — and pushes the call back onto an engineer.

The new way

Drex gives the odds. The loop makes the call.

1 · NDI
Extract the log
2 · Drex
P(flaky), not a verdict
3 · Policy
Quarantine, retry, or block
4 · Learn
Refit only if cost falls

Two thresholds, priced by cost

RETRY0.40 – 0.80

The loop doesn’t overreact. One noisy override would have raised cost from 118 to 484 engineer-minutes. The thresholds held at 0.40 / 0.80.

The takeaway

Stop asking AI
what it thinks.

Ask it how sure it is. That’s what Calibrated means.

A verdict can’t learn your costs. A probability can.
