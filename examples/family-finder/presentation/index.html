THE REALIZATION

Most pairs are simple

87%

72 of 83 pairs right with a 5-minute rule and no AI: same first name + same spouse or address, minus conflicts. Scored against a 4-model jury.

What the rule misses

Period abbreviations: Jno = John, Edw = Edward, Eliz = Elizabeth

Spelling: Wilhemenia / Wilhelmina

Job wording: cook vs dom

Conflicting clues

So: rules for the easy majority, a decision model for the messy middle, people for the truly ambiguous.

THE DESIGN

A hybrid matcher in three stages

STAGE 1 · RULES

Abbreviation table (~30 names), soundex, occupation abbreviations.

Link on same name + spouse, address or occupation. Hard no-link on conflicts with nothing else in common.

STAGE 2 · DREX MAX v1.1

Everything the rules don’t settle.

p ≥ 0.75 link · p ≤ 0.25 no link. Name-only pairs need p ≥ 0.9.

STAGE 3 · PEOPLE

The middle band goes to a family member to review.

One-to-one: each person links to at most one person in the next edition.

Check set: rules 71/71 right · Drex 9/9 right · 3 to review

On the 83 pairs where DeepSeek V4 Pro, Kimi K3.1 Pro, Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro agree.

SELF-IMPROVING LOOP

Drex’s decisions become rules

680/697

From 4,356 Drex decisions: “same name + same occupation, different address” was linked 680 times and rejected 0.

Now a rule: ~700 fewer calls per year pair.

FOUND

lndrs = laundress · slsmn = salesman · pntr = painter

REJECTED

cook ↔ dom, laborer ↔ porter: job changes, not synonyms.

CAUGHT

Where only the name matches, Drex linked 74% and the big models split → stricter 0.9 bar.

Guardrail: a fixed human-labeled check set gates every rule change.
